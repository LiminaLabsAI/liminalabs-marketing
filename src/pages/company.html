Company

A company should own the way it works.

Every decision agreed, every correction made, every job that went differently than planned is an organisation working out how it operates. Today almost none of that is kept. It lives in people's heads and walks out with them, so the next team starts from nothing and each new use case is another project from scratch.

We think that should accumulate instead — held by the company, in a form it owns, while the models underneath it come and go. That is what we are building towards.

Limina is the Latin plural of limen: thresholds. A threshold is not a wall — things cross it, in both directions — and it still marks exactly where one place stops and another begins.

What we believe

Owning your AI is not about where the model runs. It is about owning the thinking around it. The model is rented and easy to swap. What should not be swappable is what your company is trying to do, what it is allowed to do, and what it has learned. Put the limits outside the model, where it cannot talk its way around them, and the rest follows.

Who we build for

Any team that needs its people and its agents working from the same understanding — software and engineering, finance, healthcare, legal, manufacturing, plant operations. Where the work is regulated, the question is never whether AI can produce an answer but whether anyone can defend acting on it. Everywhere else, the cost of a wrong assumption is just quieter.
