Three questions

Most teams can answer one of these. Very few can answer all three.

Can you say what the AI was supposed to do — and who agreed to it?

Work arrives as a one-line request, not a proper brief. Everyone fills the gaps with their own assumptions, nobody says so, and the guesses pile up until the result is wrong. The model was never the problem.

Can you stop it doing something else — and prove what it did?

If the rules only live in the prompt, the AI can be talked around them — and your data goes out to someone else's servers. When an auditor asks what happened, you need a real answer.

Does what you learn survive the next project?

The thinking happened inside one AI call and then disappeared. The next team starts from nothing, and when an experienced person leaves, what they knew leaves with them.

Where the answer is no, you pay for it four times over: the agent makes things up, you run it again and again, your best people fix its guesses, and work that went wrong early is only spotted much later. And it repeats — every new use case starts from nothing.

If you can answer all three today, you don't need us.

The insight

Sovereignty is not about hosting weights. It is about owning your reasoning.

The model is the muscle. You rent it, and you can swap it. The mind is different: what your company is trying to do, what it is allowed to do, and what it has learned. That part should belong to you.

The model — you rent it

  • A better one comes out every few months
  • The price keeps falling
  • It can change without warning you
  • It is worth less every year

The record — you own it

  • What you asked for, and who agreed
  • The rules the AI cannot talk its way around
  • What happened, still checkable a year later
  • It is worth more every year
