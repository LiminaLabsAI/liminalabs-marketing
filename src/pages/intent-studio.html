Intent Studio · The work layer

Your people, their agents, one intent.

An intent is one piece of work — a line changeover, a migration, an audit response — written down as something a team and its agents can both work against. Intent Studio is where that gets built: questions, context and trade-offs, until it is sharp enough to act on.

Why agents fail

Agents don't fail because the model is weak. They fail because the intent was thin.

Given a vague brief, an agent does the worst thing possible: it carries on. It fills every gap with a guess, quietly and confidently. Each guess becomes the basis for the next step. By the time you see any output, it is built on guesses nobody agreed to.

Cost 01

Hallucination

The agent asserts what it was never told and could not know.

Cost 02

Iteration

Round after round of "not quite, try again" — each one a full paid run.

Cost 03

Rework

The most expensive people in the building fixing an agent's guesses.

Cost 04

Corrupted plans

Work that was wrong at step two and only visibly wrong at step twenty.

You cannot see the gap while it is still cheap to fix. You can see it clearly once it is not.

What it does

An intent is a record, not a thread.

One written intent

Eight fixed sections, so nothing important gets assumed:

At a glance · Goal · In scope · Out of scope · Definition of done · If it breaks · Systems affected · Why now

Claim states

Every line is marked Proposed, Agreed, Contested or Escalated, with a name against it. Disagreement stays visible instead of being smoothed over, and an agent cannot approve its own work.

Proposed Agreed Contested Escalated

The autonomy dial

You decide how much the agents settle alone.

Five levels, set per workspace, per type of work, per risk level and per agent. You can lower it at any time; raising it needs someone to approve. So you can let agents decide more, and still defend having let them.

LevelWhat it may do aloneWhat that requires
ProposeSuggest claims, questions and evidence. A person agrees to everything.Nothing. This is the safe floor.
Settle the immaterialClose low-consequence items alone; escalate anything material.That materiality is defined by policy, not by the model's judgement.
Settle within a domainAct unsupervised inside an intent type where its measured record supports it.A track record that is measured, and specific to that domain.
Build and prepareProduce artifacts and ready them for release without a human in each step.That the gates still hold — attestation and approval unchanged.
Act, with gatesCarry the work through, stopping only where policy demands a named human.Halt and revoke are instant, and every step is reconstructable.

Holds at every setting: attributable · grounded · bounded · revocable

How it runs

In your environment, over your models.

The workspace, the intents and the record of decisions all sit on your own servers, using whichever models you already run — with Precepta underneath or without it. It does not need anything else to be useful.

Who it's for

Any team coordinating people and agents.

Built for people and agents working together on the same job. It helps most where teams who do not share the same language have to agree — a plant, a supply chain, controls and quality, all on one change.

How it is licensed
