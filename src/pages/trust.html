Trust and security

Most vendor risk does not apply to software you run yourself.

We are early access, unfunded, and hold no certifications. This page says exactly what that does and does not mean for your assessment — and nothing on it is gated.

01

Zero-egress architecture

The boundary is a property of where the software sits, not a setting inside it. Under self-hosting, Precepta runs in your cloud account or data centre with no public route. Model inference happens in-boundary. There is no telemetry and no phone-home, so there is no channel over which your data could reach us even if we wanted it.

That is the whole argument, and it is structural: we cannot lose data we never receive.

02

What we hold, and what we don't

Under a self-hosted deployment: nothing. No prompts, no completions, no embeddings, no audit records, no customer data of any kind. Your audit store is yours and stays in your infrastructure.

The exception is the hosted sandbox, which runs on our infrastructure in {{REGION}} and is for non-production, synthetic or non-sensitive data only. Anything real belongs in a self-hosted evaluation, where nothing leaves your boundary.

03

Attestation and live probes

Precepta issues a signed sovereignty attestation, and continuous live probes test the egress boundary rather than asserting it. This is the part of the system we most want you to distrust on principle — which is why it is demonstrated during your evaluation, on your own network, rather than shown as a sample here.

Our intent is to open-source the verifier so an attestation can be checked with code we did not write. That does not exist yet, and we will not describe it as though it does.

04

Tamper-evident audit

Every request, policy decision, redaction and output is recorded with lineage, so a specific action can be reconstructed after the fact — what was asked, what policy said, what the model saw, and what came back. Integrity is chained, so a record cannot be altered after the event without the alteration being evident.

08

The two questions you were going to ask anyway

"Are you SOC 2 certified?"

Not yet — we're early access, and we'll start that as we scale. But the reason it usually matters is that a vendor holds your data. We're self-hosted with zero egress: your data never reaches us. Most of the vendor-risk surface simply isn't there. What you're assessing is software you run yourself — and we can show you how it's built, signed and patched. Here's our security overview and our vulnerability policy, and we'll happily put the source in escrow.

On the certification path: we will pursue {{ISO 27001 or SOC 2}} once we have paying customers. Certifying a product that is still changing weekly wastes money and freezes things that should still be moving.

"You're unfunded — what if you disappear?"

Fair question. It runs on your infrastructure, so it keeps running if we vanish. Everything exports in an open format, so your knowledge isn't trapped. And we'll put the source in escrow if you want the belt as well as the braces.
